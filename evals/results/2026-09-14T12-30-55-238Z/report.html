## 有个别的洞，不是 last revealer

你贴的合约里，commitment **没有绑定提交者地址**：`c = keccak256(r)`，`reveal` 只校验 `keccak256(r) == commitments[msg.sender]`。所以：

1. 诚实方 h 在提交窗口发 `commit(c_h)`，**所有人都看得见**（你自己说的，commit 实时可见）。
2. 敌手 A 也是 proposer（32 ETH 谁都能押进来），直接发 `commit(c_h)` —— 一模一样的值，合约不查重，`commitments[msg.sender] == 0` 是按地址查的，过。
3. 揭示窗口里 h 发 `reveal(r_h)`，A 看到后跟着发 `reveal(r_h)`，`keccak256(r_h) == c_h` 校验通过。`accum ^= r_h` 执行了两次，**异或自消**。

结果：`r_h` 从 `accum` 里被完全抹掉。1 诚实 + 1 敌手时 `accum = 0`；一般情况下 `accum` 只剩敌手自己选的值，完全可预测。

这条跟你划掉的两件事都不沾边：**A 全程按时正确揭示了**，罚没条件根本不触发，32 ETH 一分不罚；你「假设所有人最后都会揭示」的模型也完全成立——攻击就发生在这个模型内部。所以经济学那套在这儿没有抓手。

形式化上它卡在哪一步也很清楚：game hopping 走到「`r_h` 均匀且与其余项独立，故 XOR 均匀」那一跳时，**独立性不成立**——敌手揭示的值可以是 `r_h` 的函数（就等于它）。师兄给的思路里 hiding 那段是对的，但 hiding 只管「揭示前不知道 `r_h`」，不管「揭示后能不能把 `r_h` 复读一遍」，后者要靠 binding 到地址。

另外一个纯定义层面的问题：你现在那句「信标输出对敌手不可预测、与均匀分布不可区分」在揭示结束后是**字面为假**的——reveal 全公开，敌手把链上的值异或一遍就得到 `accum`。定义必须相对于**提交窗口结束时的 view** 来量化。下面我按这个写了。

```latex
% ===== Section 5: Security of the randomness beacon =====

\subsection{Model and assumptions}
\label{subsec:beacon-model}

We model \textsf{keccak256} as a random oracle $H:\{0,1\}^{*}\!\to\{0,1\}^{256}$.
A round fixes a proposer set $\mathcal{P}$, $n=|\mathcal{P}|\in[8,32]$, where each
$P_i$ holds a distinct address $\mathsf{addr}_i$. The adversary $\mathcal{A}$
statically corrupts $\mathcal{C}\subsetneq\mathcal{P}$; write
$\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{P}\setminus\mathcal{C}$ with $|\mathcal{H}|\ge 1$.
$\mathcal{A}$ is \emph{rushing}: it observes each commitment and each reveal as
soon as it is included and may schedule its own transactions afterwards inside
the same window. We assume:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item[\textbf{A1}] \emph{(Phase separation)} Commitments are included at height
    $\le\textsc{CommitEnd}$, reveals at height $>\textsc{CommitEnd}$; enforced on-chain.
  \item[\textbf{A2}] \emph{(Domain separation)} $c_i=H(r_i\,\|\,\mathsf{addr}_i)$, and
    \textsf{reveal} accepts $r$ from $\mathsf{addr}_i$ only if $H(r\,\|\,\mathsf{addr}_i)=c_i$.
  \item[\textbf{A3}] \emph{(Full reveal)} Every proposer that committed also reveals;
    discharged economically by the slashing rule of \S\ref{sec:incentives}.
\end{itemize}

Unpredictability must be stated relative to $\mathcal{A}$'s view at
$\textsc{CommitEnd}$: reveals are public, so after $\textsc{REVEALEND}$ the output
is trivially computable by everyone. This is the point at which the round's
randomness is already determined but not yet known.

\begin{definition}[Beacon unpredictability]
\label{def:beacon}
For a beacon $\Pi$ and adversary $\mathcal{A}$ define
$\mathbf{Exp}^{\mathrm{pred}}_{\Pi,\mathcal{A}}$:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
  \item Each $P_i\in\mathcal{H}$ samples $r_i\!\gets\!\{0,1\}^{256}$ and publishes
        $c_i=H(r_i\|\mathsf{addr}_i)$.
  \item $\mathcal{A}$, with oracle access to $H$ and given $\{c_i\}_{i\in\mathcal{H}}$,
        publishes $\{c_j\}_{j\in\mathcal{C}}$ before $\textsc{CommitEnd}$.
  \item Let $\mathsf{view}^{\mathrm{com}}_{\mathcal{A}}$ be $\mathcal{A}$'s view at height
        $\textsc{CommitEnd}$. $\mathcal{A}$ outputs a guess $y^{\star}\in\{0,1\}^{256}$.
  \item All parties reveal (A3) and the beacon outputs
        $\mathsf{accum}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{P}} r_i$.
\end{enumerate}
$\mathcal{A}$ wins iff $y^{\star}=\mathsf{accum}$, and
$\mathbf{Adv}^{\mathrm{pred}}_{\Pi,\mathcal{A}}=\Pr[\mathcal{A}\text{ wins}]$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Beacon security]
\label{thm:beacon}
Under \textnormal{A1--A3} in the random oracle model, for every $\mathcal{A}$
making at most $q$ queries to $H$ and corrupting at most $n-1$ proposers,
\[
  \mathbf{Adv}^{\mathrm{pred}}_{\Pi,\mathcal{A}}\;\le\;2^{-256}+(n+1)\,q\,2^{-256}.
\]
Moreover the distribution of $\mathsf{accum}$ conditioned on
$\mathsf{view}^{\mathrm{com}}_{\mathcal{A}}$ is within statistical distance
$(n+1)q\,2^{-256}$ of the uniform distribution on $\{0,1\}^{256}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix one honest proposer $P_h\in\mathcal{H}$ and let $S=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{P}
\setminus\{h\}}r_i$, so $\mathsf{accum}=r_h\oplus S$. We proceed by game hopping;
$\mathsf{W}_k$ denotes the event that $\mathcal{A}$ wins in $\mathbf{G}_k$.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{$\mathbf{G}_0$.} The experiment of
Definition~\ref{def:beacon}. By definition
$\Pr[\mathsf{W}_0]=\mathbf{Adv}^{\mathrm{pred}}_{\Pi,\mathcal{A}}$.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{$\mathbf{G}_1$.} $H$ is lazily sampled by a table.
This is a perfect simulation: $\Pr[\mathsf{W}_1]=\Pr[\mathsf{W}_0]$.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{$\mathbf{G}_2$ (hiding).} Replace $P_h$'s commitment by
$\hat c\gets\{0,1\}^{256}$ and defer sampling $r_h$ to the reveal step, at which
point the table is patched with $H(r_h\|\mathsf{addr}_h):=\hat c$. The games differ
only if patching fails, i.e.\ if $\mathcal{A}$ has already queried
$(r_h\|\mathsf{addr}_h)$, or has already obtained $\hat c$ as the image of some
query $(r\|\mathsf{addr}_h)$. Since $r_h$ is uniform and independent of
$\mathcal{A}$'s view in $\mathbf{G}_2$, and $\hat c$ is uniform, each event has
probability at most $q2^{-256}$, so
$|\Pr[\mathsf{W}_2]-\Pr[\mathsf{W}_1]|\le 2q2^{-256}$.
In $\mathbf{G}_2$, $\mathsf{view}^{\mathrm{com}}_{\mathcal{A}}$ is independent of $r_h$.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{$\mathbf{G}_3$ (binding).} Abort if some
$P_j\in\mathcal{C}$ reveals $r_j$ such that $(r_j\|\mathsf{addr}_j)$ was not queried
to $H$ before $\textsc{CommitEnd}$. A non-aborting-to-aborting transition requires
one of $\mathcal{A}$'s at most $q$ later queries to return one of the at most
$n-1$ corrupted commitments, which by A1 were fixed at $\textsc{CommitEnd}$;
a union bound gives
$|\Pr[\mathsf{W}_3]-\Pr[\mathsf{W}_2]|\le (n-1)q2^{-256}$.
Note that A2 is what makes this hop tight: an opening submitted from
$\mathsf{addr}_j$ must be a preimage under the domain $\mathsf{addr}_j$, so
replaying another proposer's commitment is exactly the aborting event.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Analysis of $\mathbf{G}_3$.} Conditioned on no abort,
every corrupted $r_j$ was queried before $\textsc{CommitEnd}$ and is therefore a
deterministic function of $\mathsf{view}^{\mathrm{com}}_{\mathcal{A}}$; the same
holds for the remaining honest contributions, which are fixed at that point. Hence
$S$ is determined by $\mathsf{view}^{\mathrm{com}}_{\mathcal{A}}$, while by
$\mathbf{G}_2$ the value $r_h$ is uniform and independent of it. By A3 the output
is $\mathsf{accum}=r_h\oplus S$, so for every fixing of
$\mathsf{view}^{\mathrm{com}}_{\mathcal{A}}$ and hence of $S$ and $y^{\star}$,
$\mathsf{accum}$ is uniform on $\{0,1\}^{256}$ and
$\Pr[\mathsf{W}_3]=2^{-256}$.

\smallskip Summing the hops yields
$\mathbf{Adv}^{\mathrm{pred}}_{\Pi,\mathcal{A}}\le 2^{-256}+(n+1)q2^{-256}$,
and the same chain bounds the statistical distance claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Necessity of A2]
\label{rem:domain-sep}
A2 is not a technicality. Without it ($c_i=H(r_i)$), a corrupted $P_j$ replays the
honest commitment, $c_j:=c_h$, and after observing $r_h$ on-chain reveals the same
$r_h$; both openings verify and the two contributions cancel in the XOR
accumulator. The $\mathbf{G}_3$ abort then occurs with probability $1$ rather than
$O(nq2^{-256})$, and the independence of $S$ from $r_h$ used in the final step
fails. With $|\mathcal{H}|=1$ and a single such $P_j$ the output is identically
$0$. The attack requires no withholding: every party reveals correctly and on
time, so it is not addressed by the reveal-deadline slashing of
\S\ref{sec:incentives}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Leader selection]
The leader is $\mathsf{accum}\bmod n$. For $n\le 32$ the modular reduction of a
uniform $256$-bit string is within statistical distance $n2^{-256}$ of uniform on
$[n]$, which is absorbed into the bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:beacon}.
\end{remark}
```

## 你现在这份合约，定理是假的 —— 而且跟 last revealer 无关

攻击（全程每个人都揭示，每笔交易都合法）：

1. 提交窗口有 128 个区块，敌手能先看到诚实方的 `c_h` 上链。合约里 `commit` **只检查 `commitments[msg.sender] == 0`，从不检查 `c` 这个值是否已被别人用过**。所以敌手用自己的 proposer 账户直接 `commit(c_h)`，抄一份。
2. 揭示窗口里诚实方发 `reveal(r_h)`，公开可见。敌手随后用那个账户发 `reveal(r_h)` —— `keccak256(r_h) == commitments[A]` 成立，检查通过。
3. `accum ^= r_h` 执行了两次。**`r_h ^ r_h = 0`，诚实方的熵被整除掉了。**

结果：`accum` 等于敌手自己那些 `r_j` 的异或，全是它自己选的、自己知道的值。只有一个敌手账户时 `accum` 恒等于 0。leader 选举完全可预测，且只要它最后揭示就是可指定的。

这不是 last revealer：没有人拒绝揭示，你「假设所有人最后都会揭示」这条模型假设**盖不住它**。师兄那句「未知均匀随机值 XOR 上任何别的东西还是均匀的」漏掉的前提正是**独立性** —— 抄提交让敌手的贡献跟 `r_h` 完全相关。形式化写下去，卡死的就是这一步，写不过去。

**一行修复**：把提交的原像做域分隔 —— `c_i = keccak256(abi.encodePacked(r_i, msg.sender, roundId))`，`reveal` 里同样用 `msg.sender` 重算。抄来的 `c_h` 换个地址就再也打不开了。（或者加 `mapping(bytes32=>bool) used` 拒重复提交，同样一行。）

下面 LaTeX 我按**带域分隔**的版本写，定理是真的、证明能走通。我的建议是论文就写这个版本，合约的修复排在你赶得上的时候 —— 另外这是主网上的真实可利用漏洞，不管论文怎么处理，值得单独知会运维那边。

## Section 5 的 LaTeX

```latex
% ---- Section 5: formal analysis of the commit--reveal beacon ----
% Preamble needs amsmath + amsthm and:
%   \newtheorem{definition}{Definition}[section]
%   \newtheorem{theorem}[definition]{Theorem}
%   \newtheorem{corollary}[definition]{Corollary}
%   \newtheorem{remark}[definition]{Remark}
\newcommand{\rs}{\xleftarrow{\$}}

\subsection{Model}
Let $H:\{0,1\}^{*}\!\to\{0,1\}^{256}$ be modelled as a random oracle.
A round is identified by a tag $\rho$ and run by a proposer set $P$ with
$8\le|P|=n\le 32$, fixed before the commit window opens. Proposer $i$ samples
$r_i\rs\{0,1\}^{256}$, posts $c_i\gets H(r_i\,\|\,i\,\|\,\rho)$ during the commit
window, opens it with $r_i$ during the reveal window --- an opening $(r,i)$ being
accepted iff $H(r\,\|\,i\,\|\,\rho)=c_i$ --- and the beacon output is
$\Omega\gets\bigoplus_{i\in P}r_i$. The leader of the next round is
$\Omega \bmod n$.

The adversary $\mathcal{A}$ statically corrupts $C\subsetneq P$ with
$P\setminus C\neq\emptyset$, and is \emph{rushing}: it observes every commitment
on chain before posting its own, and every honest opening before posting its own.
We assume \emph{complete opening}: every $i\in P$ posts a valid opening, enforced
out of band by the $32$\,ETH bond, which is slashed on a missing reveal.

\begin{definition}[Beacon indistinguishability]\label{def:beacon}
For a rushing adversary $\mathcal{A}$ making at most $q$ random-oracle queries,
define $\mathbf{G}_0$:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
  \item For each $h\in P\setminus C$: $r_h\rs\{0,1\}^{256}$,
        $c_h\gets H(r_h\|h\|\rho)$; the $c_h$ are handed to $\mathcal{A}$.
  \item $\mathcal{A}$ adaptively posts $\{c_j\}_{j\in C}$, then receives
        $\{r_h\}_{h\in P\setminus C}$ and adaptively posts its openings.
  \item $\Omega\gets\bigoplus_{i\in P}r_i$ over all accepted openings;
        $b\rs\{0,1\}$; $\mathcal{A}$ is given $\Omega$ if $b=1$ and
        $\Omega^{*}\rs\{0,1\}^{256}$ otherwise, and outputs $\hat b$.
\end{enumerate}
Let $S_0$ be the event $b=\hat b$ and
$\mathsf{Adv}^{\mathsf{beac}}(\mathcal{A}):=\bigl|\Pr[S_0]-\tfrac12\bigr|$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:beacon}
In the random-oracle model, for every rushing $\mathcal{A}$ corrupting
$C\subsetneq P$ with at least one honest proposer and making at most $q$ queries,
\[
  \mathsf{Adv}^{\mathsf{beac}}(\mathcal{A})\;\le\;\frac{q^{2}}{2^{257}}+\frac{q}{2^{255}} \;=\; \mathrm{negl}(\lambda).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix one honest $h\in P\setminus C$; all games share a probability space.

\smallskip\noindent
$\mathbf{G}_0\to\mathbf{G}_1$ \emph{(failure event).} $\mathbf{G}_1$ aborts on
$F_1$: two distinct oracle queries collide, or some accepted opening $(r_j,j)$,
$j\in C$, is such that $r_j\|j\|\rho$ was not queried by $\mathcal{A}$ before the
commit window closed. Absent a collision, an accepted opening for $j$ forces the
preimage to carry the tag $j\|\rho$; since $j\neq h$, the honest query
$r_h\|h\|\rho$ is a different oracle point and cannot be reused. Guessing an
unqueried preimage succeeds with probability at most $2^{-256}$ per commitment,
so $\Pr[F_1]\le q^{2}/2^{257}+q/2^{256}$. By the Difference Lemma,
$|\Pr[S_0]-\Pr[S_1]|\le\Pr[F_1]$. In $\mathbf{G}_1$ every adversarial $r_j$ is
fixed by the end of the commit window; with complete opening,
$X:=\bigoplus_{i\neq h}r_i$ is therefore determined before any honest value
is released.

\smallskip\noindent
$\mathbf{G}_1\to\mathbf{G}_2$ \emph{(bridging + failure event).} $\mathbf{G}_2$
answers $c_h$ with a fresh uniform value and defers sampling $r_h$ until the
reveal window. The two games differ only on $F_2$: $\mathcal{A}$ queries
$r_h\|h\|\rho$ before that point. As $r_h$ is uniform on $\{0,1\}^{256}$ and
independent of $\mathcal{A}$'s view, $\Pr[F_2]\le q/2^{256}$, hence
$|\Pr[S_1]-\Pr[S_2]|\le q/2^{256}$.

\smallskip\noindent
$\mathbf{G}_2\to\mathbf{G}_3$ \emph{(one-time pad).} In $\mathbf{G}_2$,
$\Omega=r_h\oplus X$ with $X$ fixed before $r_h$ is sampled, so $r_h$ is uniform
and independent of $X$ and $\Omega$ is uniform on $\{0,1\}^{256}$. Replacing
$\Omega$ by $\Omega^{*}\rs\{0,1\}^{256}$ in $\mathbf{G}_3$ is a perfect
rewrite: $\Pr[S_2]=\Pr[S_3]$. Both branches of $b$ are now identically
distributed, so $\Pr[S_3]=\tfrac12$. Summing the three transitions gives the
bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
Any such $\mathcal{A}$ predicts $\Omega$ before the reveal window with
probability at most $2^{-256}+q^{2}/2^{257}+q/2^{255}$, and predicts the next
leader with probability at most $1/n+n/2^{256}+q^{2}/2^{257}+q/2^{255}$, the
$n/2^{256}$ term bounding the modular bias of $\Omega\bmod n$ for $n\le 32$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Domain separation is necessary, not cosmetic]
Binding the proposer identity into the preimage is what makes transition
$\mathbf{G}_0\to\mathbf{G}_1$ go through. Under the plain variant
$c_i=H(r_i)$, a corrupt proposer republishes an honest $c_h$ as its own
commitment and, being rushing, replays the honest opening $r_h$; both openings
are accepted, $r_h\oplus r_h=0$, and the honest contribution is annihilated
without any party withholding a reveal. Then $\Pr[F_1]=1$ and the bound is
vacuous --- correctly so, since the beacon is fully predictable in that case.
\end{remark}
```

which the slashing rule of \S\ref{sec:incentives} enforces. The round output is
$\Omega=\bigoplus_{i\in P} r_{i}$.

\begin{definition}[Commitment freshness]\label{def:fresh}
An execution is \emph{fresh} if no corrupted proposer publishes a commitment already published by an
honest one, i.e.\ $c_{j}\neq c_{i}$ for all $j\in C$, $i\in H$. $\mathcal{A}$ is fresh if it produces
fresh executions with probability $1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Beacon pseudorandomness]\label{def:beacon}
For $\mathcal{A}=(\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{cm}},\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{rv}},\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{ds}})$
sharing state and $b\in\{0,1\}$, let $\mathbf{G}^{b}_{\mathcal{A}}(\lambda)$ be:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{Commit.} Each $i\in H$ draws $r_{i}\gets\{0,1\}^{\lambda}$ and publishes
        $c_{i}=\mathcal{H}(r_{i})$; then $\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathsf{cm}}(\{c_{i}\}_{i\in H})$
        publishes $\{c_{j}\}_{j\in C}$.
  \item \emph{Reveal.} The openings $\{r_{i}\}_{i\in H}$ are published; then
        $\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathsf{rv}}(\{r_{i}\}_{i\in H})$ publishes, for every $j\in C$,
        an opening $r_{j}$ accepted iff $\mathcal{H}(r_{j})=c_{j}$. Set
        $\Omega_{0}=\bigoplus_{i\in P}r_{i}$.
  \item \emph{Distinguish.} $\Omega_{1}\gets\{0,1\}^{\lambda}$;
        output $b'\gets\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathsf{ds}}(\Omega_{b})$.
\end{enumerate}
Step~2 gives $\mathcal{A}$ the honest openings before its own, reflecting that reveals are public on
chain in real time. $\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{ds}}$ runs on the state left by $\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{cm}}$
and is \emph{not} given the honest openings: it models a party that must act on $\Omega$ while $\Omega$
is still hidden, i.e.\ at the close of the commit window, the last point at which any party can still
influence the round. We write
$\mathbf{Adv}^{\mathrm{bcn}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\lambda)=
\bigl|\Pr[\mathbf{G}^{0}_{\mathcal{A}}=1]-\Pr[\mathbf{G}^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}=1]\bigr|$.
\end{definition}

proposer set with $H\neq\emptyset$,
\[

\]
negligible in $\lambda$ for p.p.t.\ $\mathcal{A}$. In particular, at the close of the commit window
$\mathcal{A}$ predicts $\Omega$ with probability at most $2^{-\lambda}+4(q+n)^{2}2^{-\lambda}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\mathbf{G}_{0}$ is $\mathbf{G}^{0}_{\mathcal{A}}$.

some such $r_{i}$. Until $\mathsf{bad}_{1}$ the games are identically distributed, since the image of an
unqueried point is uniform and independent of $\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{cm}}$'s view. At most $q+n$ points
are defined at programming time, so $\Pr[\mathsf{bad}_{1}]\le n(q+n)/2^{\lambda}$. Note
$\mathsf{bad}_{1}$ also excludes collisions among the $r_{i}$, hence no honest term cancels in $\Omega$.

$\mathbf{G}_{2}$ aborts on $\mathsf{bad}_{2}$: some accepted $r_{j}$, $j\in C$, was not queried to
$\mathcal{H}$ before the end of the commit phase. This is where freshness enters. By
Definition~\ref{def:fresh} we have $\mathcal{H}(r_{i})=c_{i}\neq c_{j}$ for every $i\in H$, so the
openings handed to $\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{rv}}$ in step~2 are not valid openings of any corrupt
commitment; every other point $\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{rv}}$ can name is either never queried, whose image
is uniform and matches $c_{j}$ with probability $2^{-\lambda}$, or a reveal-phase query, which hits the
fixed set $\{c_{j}\}_{j\in C}$ with probability at most $n2^{-\lambda}$. Summing over $\le n$ openings
and $\le q$ queries, $\Pr[\mathsf{bad}_{2}]\le n(q+1)/2^{\lambda}$.

$\mathbf{G}_{3}$ aborts on $\mathsf{bad}_{3}$, a collision among $\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{cm}}$'s queries:

$r_{i}$ are drawn, and is independent of $S_{H}=\bigoplus_{i\in H}r_{i}$.

$\mathbf{G}_{4}$ replaces $\Omega_{0}$ by a uniform $\lambda$-bit string. Each $r_{i}$ is uniform on the
at least $2^{\lambda}-(q+n)$ points where $\mathcal{H}$ is undefined, so $S_{H}$ is within statistical

\end{proof}

\paragraph{Model.}
We analyse a single beacon round in the random oracle model, modelling
$\mathsf{keccak256}$ as a random oracle $\mathcal{O}:\{0,1\}^{*}\to\{0,1\}^{256}$.

$c_h=\mathcal{O}(r_h)$ during the commit window and posts $r_h$ during the reveal window.
$\mathcal{A}$ is \emph{rushing}: it observes every commitment and every reveal as soon as it
is included, and may place its own transactions in the same block. As in
Section~\ref{sec:model} every proposer opens its commitment, so the round output is
$\mathsf{out}=\bigoplus_{i\in P}r_i$, where $r_i$ is the value opened by proposer $i$.
Let $\mathsf{view}^{\mathsf{com}}_{\mathcal{A}}$ denote $\mathcal{A}$'s view at the end of the
commit window: all posted commitments, its own coins, and its $\mathcal{O}$-transcript.
By binding, $\mathsf{out}$ is already determined at that point.

\begin{definition}[Beacon pseudorandomness]
\label{def:beacon-pr}
For $P$, an adversary $\mathcal{A}$ corrupting $C\subsetneq P$ and a bit $b\in\{0,1\}$,
define $\mathsf{Exp}^{b}_{\mathcal{A},P}$:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
  \item the challenger runs the commit window with $\mathcal{A}$, playing the honest
        proposers $H$ as specified above;
  \item it sets $z_{0}=\bigoplus_{i\in P}r_{i}$, where $r_{i}$ is the unique
        $\mathcal{O}$-preimage of $c_{i}$ (the experiment returns $0$ if some $c_{i}$ has
        no unique preimage), and samples $z_{1}\overset{\$}{\leftarrow}\{0,1\}^{256}$;
  \item $\mathcal{A}$ receives $z_{b}$ and outputs a bit, which the experiment returns.
\end{enumerate}
The beacon is \emph{$\varepsilon$-pseudorandom} against $\mathcal{A}$ if
$\mathsf{Adv}(\mathcal{A}):=\bigl|\Pr[\mathsf{Exp}^{0}_{\mathcal{A},P}=1]
-\Pr[\mathsf{Exp}^{1}_{\mathcal{A},P}=1]\bigr|\le\varepsilon$.
\end{definition}

The challenge is issued at the end of the commit window: once the reveal window closes every
observer recomputes $\mathsf{out}$, so pseudorandomness can only be demanded of the interval in
which the output is already fixed but not yet opened.

\begin{definition}[Surviving honest contribution]
\label{def:survival}
A round transcript \emph{preserves an honest contribution} if there exists $h^{\star}\in H$
such that $\#\{i\in P:c_{i}=c_{h^{\star}}\}$ is odd.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:majority}
If $|H|>|C|$, then every transcript preserves an honest contribution, except with probability
at most $\binom{|H|}{2}2^{-256}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Honest commitments are images of independent uniform preimages, hence pairwise distinct except
with the stated probability. Each corrupted proposer posts one commitment and therefore matches
at most one honest commitment. With $|C|<|H|$ some $c_{h^{\star}}$, $h^{\star}\in H$, is matched
by no corrupted proposer and thus has multiplicity exactly $1$.
\end{proof}

\[

\]

$\mathsf{G}_{j}$ returns $1$.

\smallskip\noindent
$\mathsf{G}_{0}$: the experiment $\mathsf{Exp}^{0}_{\mathcal{A},P}$.

\smallskip\noindent
$\mathsf{G}_{1}$: as $\mathsf{G}_{0}$, but abort if two distinct points of the
$\mathcal{O}$-transcript (including the honest queries) collide. There are at most $q+n$ such
points, so $|\Pr[\mathsf{G}_{0}]-\Pr[\mathsf{G}_{1}]|\le\binom{q+n}{2}2^{-256}\le(q+n)^{2}2^{-257}$.

the end of the commit window, honest commitments are pairwise distinct, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:majority} some $h^{\star}\in H$ has $c_{h^{\star}}$ of odd multiplicity.

\textsc{Keccak-256}, modelled as a random oracle.  Each proposer $i$ draws
$r_i\gets\$\,\{0,1\}^{256}$, posts $c_i=H(r_i)$ during the commit window and
$r_i$ during the reveal window; the beacon output is the final accumulator value

\begin{description}
\item[\textnormal{(A1)}] \textbf{Static corruption, one honest proposer.}
$\mathcal{A}$ fixes a corrupt set $\mathcal{C}\subsetneq\mathcal{P}$ before the
round starts, and $\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{P}\setminus\mathcal{C}$ satisfies
$|\mathcal{H}|\ge 1$.

\item[\textnormal{(A2)}] \textbf{Rushing adversary.}  Commits and reveals are
ordinary L2 transactions, so $\mathcal{A}$ observes each message as soon as it is
included and may schedule its own messages, within the same window, after any
honest message.

\item[\textnormal{(A3)}] \textbf{No abort.}  Every proposer that committed also
reveals before \texttt{REVEAL\_END}.  This is enforced out of band by slashing
the $32$\,ETH stake of a non-revealer; withholding strategies are therefore
outside the model.

\item[\textnormal{(A4)}] \textbf{Distinct commitments.}  The contract rejects a
commitment equal to one already recorded in the current round, so
$c_i\neq c_j$ for all $i\neq j\in\mathcal{P}$.
\end{description}

\subsection{Definition}

\begin{definition}[Beacon indistinguishability]
\label{def:beacon}
For a bit $b$ and an adversary $\mathcal{A}$, let
$\mathsf{Exp}^{\mathrm{bcn}}_{b}(\mathcal{A})$ be the following experiment.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item $\mathcal{A}$ outputs $\mathcal{C}\subsetneq\mathcal{P}$ with
      $\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{P}\setminus\mathcal{C}\neq\emptyset$.
\item For each $h\in\mathcal{H}$: $r_h\gets\$\,\{0,1\}^{256}$,
      $c_h:=H(r_h)$.  The $c_h$ are handed to $\mathcal{A}$.
\item \emph{Commit phase.}  $\mathcal{A}$ outputs $\{c_a\}_{a\in\mathcal{C}}$,
      subject to \textnormal{(A4)}.
\item \emph{Reveal phase.}  In an order chosen adaptively by $\mathcal{A}$, each
      $h\in\mathcal{H}$ discloses $r_h$ and each $a\in\mathcal{C}$ discloses some
      $r_a$ with $H(r_a)=c_a$; by \textnormal{(A2)} $\mathcal{A}$ may fix each
      $r_a$ after seeing any subset of the honest reveals, and by
      \textnormal{(A3)} every $r_a$ is disclosed.
\item $\Omega_0:=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{P}}r_i$ and
      $\Omega_1\gets\$\,\{0,1\}^{256}$; $\mathcal{A}$ receives $\Omega_b$ and
      outputs a bit $b'$.
\end{enumerate}
The advantage of $\mathcal{A}$ is
$\mathbf{Adv}^{\mathrm{bcn}}(\mathcal{A})=
\bigl|\Pr[\mathsf{Exp}^{\mathrm{bcn}}_{0}(\mathcal{A})=1]-
       \Pr[\mathsf{Exp}^{\mathrm{bcn}}_{1}(\mathcal{A})=1]\bigr|$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Security}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:beacon}
Under \textnormal{(A1)--(A4)}, for every adversary $\mathcal{A}$ making at most
$q$ queries to $H$,
\[
  \mathbf{Adv}^{\mathrm{bcn}}(\mathcal{A})\;\le\;
  \frac{2n\,(q+n)}{2^{256}}\;+\;\frac{(q+n)^{2}}{2^{257}}
  \;=\;O\!\left(\frac{(q+n)^{2}}{2^{256}}\right).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We proceed by a sequence of games, writing $\Pr[G_i]$ for the probability that
$\mathcal{A}$ outputs $1$ in game $G_i$.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{$G_0$.}  The experiment
$\mathsf{Exp}^{\mathrm{bcn}}_{0}(\mathcal{A})$ of
Definition~\ref{def:beacon}.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{$G_1$ (lazy honest commitments).}  Each honest
commitment is replaced by a uniform string $c_h\gets\$\,\{0,1\}^{256}$; the
value $r_h$ is sampled only when $h$ reveals, at which point the oracle is
programmed with $H(r_h):=c_h$.  Programming fails only if $r_h$ already
occurs among the at most $q+n$ points at which $H$ is defined, or if $c_h$
already occurs in the image, so
$|\Pr[G_1]-\Pr[G_0]|\le n(q+n)/2^{256}$.  After this hop the commit-phase view
of $\mathcal{A}$ is independent of $\{r_h\}_{h\in\mathcal{H}}$.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{$G_2$ (binding).}  Let $Q$ be the set of points
queried by $\mathcal{A}$ up to the end of the commit window.  $G_2$ aborts if
$\mathcal{A}$ reveals some $r_a\notin Q$, or if two distinct queried points
collide under $H$.  For the first event, $\mathcal{A}$ must exhibit a preimage
of $c_a$ that it did not hold at commit time.  Its only new source of preimages
during the reveal phase is the programming step of $G_1$, which yields a
preimage of $c_h$ --- and by \textnormal{(A4)} $c_a\neq c_h$ for every
$h\in\mathcal{H}$, so this is of no use; any other candidate succeeds with
probability at most $2^{-256}$ per oracle query.  Hence
$|\Pr[G_2]-\Pr[G_1]|\le nq/2^{256}+(q+n)^{2}/2^{257}$.  In $G_2$ every $r_a$ is
the unique preimage of $c_a$, and is therefore fixed at the end of the commit
window; the rushing power granted by \textnormal{(A2)} is vacuous.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{$G_3$ (uniform output).}  $\Omega$ is replaced by a
uniform string.  Fix any $h^{*}\in\mathcal{H}$, which exists by
\textnormal{(A1)}, and write $\Omega=r_{h^{*}}\oplus X$ with
$X=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{P}\setminus\{h^{*}\}}r_i$.  By $G_2$ the corrupt
summands are determined by $\mathcal{A}$'s commit-phase view, which by $G_1$ is
independent of $r_{h^{*}}$; the remaining honest summands are independently
uniform.  Thus $X$ is independent of $r_{h^{*}}$, and since $r_{h^{*}}$ is
uniform on $\{0,1\}^{256}$ so is $r_{h^{*}}\oplus X$.  The two games are
identically distributed, so $\Pr[G_3]=\Pr[G_2]$.

\medskip\noindent
$G_3$ is exactly $\mathsf{Exp}^{\mathrm{bcn}}_{1}(\mathcal{A})$, and summing the
three bounds gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Unpredictability]
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:beacon}, the probability that
$\mathcal{A}$ outputs $\Omega$ before the end of the reveal window is at most
$2^{-256}+O((q+n)^{2}/2^{256})$.  Consequently the elected leader
$\Omega\bmod n$ is within statistical distance $n/2^{256}+O((q+n)^{2}/2^{256})$
of uniform on $\mathcal{P}$, the first term accounting for modulo bias when
$n$ is not a power of two.
\end{corollary}

\end{remark}

We model \textsc{keccak256} as a random oracle $\mathcal{O}\colon\{0,1\}^{*}\to\{0,1\}^{256}$,

Let $P$ be the proposer set of the current round, $n=\lvert P\rvert\in[8,32]$.
$\mathcal{A}$ statically corrupts $C\subsetneq P$; we write $H=P\setminus C$ and assume

$\mathcal{A}$ is \emph{rushing}: it observes every commitment and every opening at the

\begin{definition}[Non-duplicating commit phase]
\label{def:nondup}
A commit-phase transcript is \emph{non-duplicating} if for every $i\in H$ and every
$j\in P\setminus\{i\}$ the posted commitments satisfy $c_j\neq c_i$.
\end{definition}

Since a valid opening of $c_j$ is a preimage of $c_j$ under $\mathcal{O}$,
Definition~\ref{def:nondup} is equivalent to requiring that each honest contribution be
XOR-ed into \texttt{accum} an odd number of times, i.e.\ that it not be cancelled.

\begin{definition}[Beacon unpredictability]
\label{def:unpred}
For a beacon protocol $\Pi$ and adversary $\mathcal{A}$, define
$\mathbf{Exp}^{\mathrm{pred}}_{\Pi,\mathcal{A}}(\lambda)$:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
  \item $\mathcal{A}$ chooses $C\subsetneq P$ with $P\setminus C\neq\emptyset$.
  \item Each $i\in H$ samples $r_i\leftarrow_{\$}\{0,1\}^{256}$ and posts
        $c_i=\mathcal{O}(r_i)$. $\mathcal{A}$ posts the commitments of $C$, adaptively and
        in any interleaving with the honest ones.
  \item At the end of the commit window $\mathcal{A}$ outputs a guess
        $b\in\{0,1\}^{256}$.
  \item The reveal window is executed and
        $\mathsf{out}=\bigoplus_{i\in P} r_i$ is the beacon output.
        The experiment returns $1$ iff $b=\mathsf{out}$.
\end{enumerate}
$\Pi$ is \emph{unpredictable} if for all PPT $\mathcal{A}$,
$\Pr[\mathbf{Exp}^{\mathrm{pred}}_{\Pi,\mathcal{A}}(\lambda)=1]\le 2^{-256}+\mathsf{negl}(\lambda)$.

window closes. The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:unpred} in fact establishes the stronger
statement that, conditioned on the entire commit-phase view of $\mathcal{A}$,
$\mathsf{out}$ is uniform on $\{0,1\}^{256}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:unpred}
In the random oracle model, if $\lvert H\rvert\ge 1$ and the commit phase is
non-duplicating in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:nondup}, then for every $\mathcal{A}$
making at most $q$ oracle queries,
\[
\Pr\!\left[\mathbf{Exp}^{\mathrm{pred}}_{\Pi,\mathcal{A}}(\lambda)=1\right]
\;\le\; 2^{-256}+\frac{2qn+q^{2}+n^{2}}{2^{256}}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $h\in H$. We proceed by a sequence of games, writing $S_k$ for the event that
game $\mathbf{G}_k$ returns $1$.

\smallskip\noindent
$\mathbf{G}_0$: the experiment of Definition~\ref{def:unpred}.

\smallskip\noindent
$\mathbf{G}_1$: identical to $\mathbf{G}_0$, except that the game aborts if any of the
following occurs: $(E_1)$ $\mathcal{A}$ queries $\mathcal{O}$ at $r_i$ for some $i\in H$
before $i$'s opening is posted; $(E_2)$ two distinct oracle queries collide;
$(E_3)$ some $c_j$, $j\in C$, is opened with a value first queried after the commit window
closed; $(E_4)$ $r_i=r_{i'}$ for distinct $i,i'\in H$. As each $r_i$ is uniform and
independent of $\mathcal{A}$'s view up to its opening, $\Pr[E_1]\le qn/2^{256}$; a birthday
bound gives $\Pr[E_2]\le q^{2}/2^{257}$; a late query hits a fixed target commitment with
probability $2^{-256}$, so $\Pr[E_3]\le qn/2^{256}$; and $\Pr[E_4]\le n^{2}/2^{257}$.
Hence
$\lvert\Pr[S_0]-\Pr[S_1]\rvert\le (2qn+q^{2}+n^{2})/2^{256}$.

\smallskip\noindent
$\mathbf{G}_2$: we defer the sampling of $r_h$. At commit time the game posts a uniform
$c_h\leftarrow_{\$}\{0,1\}^{256}$; when $h$'s opening is due it samples
$r_h\leftarrow_{\$}\{0,1\}^{256}$ and programs $\mathcal{O}(r_h):=c_h$. Conditioned on
$\neg E_1$ the point $r_h$ was never queried and, conditioned on $\neg E_2$, programming
never overwrites a previous answer, so the two games are perfectly indistinguishable:
$\Pr[S_2]=\Pr[S_1]$. This is the step that formalises hiding: in $\mathbf{G}_2$ the
commitment $c_h$ carries no information about $r_h$.

\smallskip\noindent
Now analyse $\mathbf{G}_2$. The multiset of commitments is fixed when the commit window
closes. By $\neg E_2$ and $\neg E_3$, every $c_j$ with $j\neq h$ admits exactly one opening
known to its poster, and that opening is a deterministic function of the commit-phase
view; by Definition~\ref{def:nondup} none of these commitments equals $c_h$, so no party
other than $h$ opens to $r_h$, and by $\neg E_4$ no other honest value equals $r_h$
either. Write $\mathsf{out}=r_h\oplus S$ with $S=\bigoplus_{j\in P\setminus\{h\}}r_j$.
Both $S$ and the guess $b$ are fixed before $r_h$ is sampled and are independent of it.
For any fixed $S$ and $b$, uniformity of $r_h$ gives
$\Pr[r_h\oplus S=b]=2^{-256}$, hence $\Pr[S_2]=2^{-256}$. Combining the bounds yields the
claim.

\end{corollary}
